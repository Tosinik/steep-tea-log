Home is the present tense

Home keeps the card stack, sheds the washi band, and gets a job it can be held to. The board draws the fixed spine and four states; the stack below the spine stays the user's. Reference ids — 5a Earlier today · 4a Niklas's Home · 4b the same at night · 4c day one · 4d thin.

This is a revision against a lock, not a first draft. Home was boarded in R2 WS2, deep-dived, locked at 2a, and shipped in v3.65 — DESIGN.md:6 records it. R61 protects that lock, so what follows revises where the world around Home has moved since: a fifth tab, Origins pinned to Insights, the four-lane method control, the freshness model, and a liquor swatch about to acquire a real data model. Nothing here re-opens 2a itself.

FINDINGS — WHERE THE BRIEF DISAGREES WITH THE FILES
HOME HAS
TWO BOARDS

Home is not unboarded. R2 WS2 gave it a three-direction board (2a greeting-led · 2b today & shelf · 2c bare glance), a deep-dive with morning, dark and evening-rested states, and Niklas locked 2a — greeting as Shippori hero, stat grid shed to Insights and Wrapped. Bundle 1 then drew it as a canonical screen. What Home lacks is an R4 revision board, which is a smaller and more answerable claim. Two of the four questions below were already ruled in R2; this board carries those forward rather than re-opening them.

Q1 IS
ALREADY SHUT

"Is Home a card stack at all?" was answered in R2's own framing — "Home stays a user-editable card layout — so these are cards, not a fixed page" — locked with 2a and protected since by R61. Re-asking it in R4 would reverse a lock on no new evidence. The live question is the one underneath it: what does a board draw when the user composes the surface? Answer below.

A CONTRACT
BREAKS

Clay is one committing action per screen. Cards move between screens. So a card cannot carry clay: the Wrapped teaser is clay on Insights, and the moment Niklas drags it to Home it either brings a second clay onto a surface that has one, or changes appearance on arrival. Clay belongs to surfaces, not to cards. This is not a Home problem — it is a movable-card problem the contract never met, and it is load-bearing for R4's swatch work too.

The ruling

1

Home's job: what now.

Sessions is the diary, Insights is the reflection, Wrapped is the season, Teas is the shelf as objects. All of those are past tense or object tense. Home is the only present-tense surface in the app — what is ready, what is running out, what you were in the middle of. That is a job a lobby doesn't have, and it is testable.

2

The default-set rule, and its limit.

A card defaults to Home if removing it would leave you unable to answer what now without navigating. Running low passes. Favourites passes — it is what you reach for. Earlier today passes, added by ruling 8. Sessions this week fails: it is a count of what already happened, and it is the fourth default today. The rule governs the default set only; a user's move always wins and is never second-guessed by the design. Niklas has This week, mostly and Wrapped on his Home, and both are drawn here as first-class.

3

Spine and stack — what a board can draw.

The greeting is not a card. It is the masthead — fixed, unmovable, unhideable, the app's voice and the one thing every Home has in common. Everything below it is stack, and the board draws it as one configuration, labelled as one. That is the answer to "no board can draw a user-composed surface": draw the spine as specification, draw the stack as an example.

4

The washi band: dropped.

The probation contract was "if it ever fights the masthead, drop it without ceremony." Ruling 3 makes the greeting the masthead, so the band is now a second masthead gesture stacked on the first — that is the fight, arriving on schedule. Two further reasons: D's material moments survived because they carry information (liquor = identity, clay = commit) and this one was flagged from the start as carrying none; and a texture that appears on exactly one surface can never teach what it means. Home loses nothing structural. Toggle it back on in Tweaks to see what is being given up.

5

Empty is not a stack of empty cards.

Day one, every default card has nothing in it. Four empty cards would be four apologies. A card is absent until it has something to say — so day one is the greeting and one door, and cards arrive as they earn a line. R2's dashed "add a card" slots stay, but they belong to a furnished Home in edit mode, not to a bare one: an invitee should not be asked to arrange a room before they have furniture.

6

Clay lives in the spine. Cards never carry it. Revised

The earlier ruling gave Home no committing action. That was a consequence of the broken contract, not a decision, and it cost the thing the R1 mock got right — Start steeping as the screen's one commit. Corrected: each surface's spine may carry at most one clay action; a card carries none, on any surface. Home's is the greeting's suggestion, made committing. The raised Log stays jade — it is chrome, shared by every tab, and reads as a different register from content. Caught by this rule: an earlier draft of this board painted the header avatar clay, which put a second clay on every populated Home. The locked masthead avatar is band plus a hairline, which is why it never collided — restored.

Why not "the surface that owns the card". That version is close and keeps Insights shipping unchanged, but it demotes a card at the moment the user promotes it: drag Wrapped onto Home — the first surface you see — and it renders quieter than it did on Insights. The user's own act of promotion makes the thing dimmer. Spine-only also removes the surprise entirely, since no card changes appearance when moved.

What it costs, and why the cost is right. Insights loses its clay: the Wrapped teaser becomes a quiet card. Clay was defined as one committing action, never selection — and a link to a reflective view commits nothing. That teaser was clay as emphasis, which is the misuse the contract existed to prevent. Insights then carries no clay at all, correctly: the reflective room has nothing to commit. Owed back: the teaser is Insights' warmest element and losing clay should not make it cold — that treatment is an Insights decision, not one to settle on the Home board.

7

Content bleeds to the edges. Every tab, not just Home. New

Porcelain paints edge to edge with no page container. The decisive reason is nesting: the stack below the greeting is already made of cards, and a card stack inside a card is a page inside a page. The masthead settles it too — a masthead is the top of a page, not an element floating on one.

Figure and ground still resolve, without a page edge: porcelain #F6F2E9 is the ground, surface #FFFEFB cards are the figure, and the masthead is ink on bare ground. That one-step token pair is what it was for. The "whiter" reading is a board artifact — the phone frames float on a dark surround so they read as objects, which the app never does.

This binds every tab. Home-only would give the app two container models, and the second one gets "fixed" later by someone who does not know it was chosen.

8

"Earlier today" is restored from the lock, not invented. Corrected

A correction first, and it is mine. I answered this as a new card. It is not: EARLIER TODAY already exists on the locked R3 Home — Bundle 1, the reconciled Direction-D canonical screen — as a mono section header over a hairline with liquor-swatch rows, per-origin CJK script and a fav leaf. The gap is between the lock and the build: it was designed and never implemented, which is why the shipped app leaves recent on Insights and nothing on Home. So this ruling restores a locked section; it does not add a card to the design. Same failure mode as the sprite and the tab bar — asserted from memory with the source two files away.

The gap is real and ruling 1 is what proves it: today's sittings are present tense, not past. A cup from this morning is live context for the next one — same day, same shelf, sometimes the same leaves. Ruling 1's third clause already said it: what you were in the middle of. So it belongs on Home, and the greeting knowing what the screen won't show is a defect, not a taste question.

Not by rescoping recent, though — that is shape 1, and ruling 6 just outlawed it. A card scoped to today on Home and to the last four on Insights is a card that changes what it means depending on where it sits, which is the exact fault I rejected "the surface that owns the card" for. Cards must render the same everywhere.

Two views over one table — and the lock already says so. recent = last four sittings, any date, a log, Insights. EARLIER TODAY = this calendar day only, a diary page, Home. Bundle 1 drew them as separate things; that is the justification, not an argument I need to make. Its day boundary is the greeting's day boundary — they must agree, or the masthead says "second pour today" over a section showing one row.

One deliberate departure from the lock: it becomes a card. Bundle 1 puts EARLIER TODAY in the fixed page flow, alongside the masthead. Ruling 5 came later and makes absence the mechanism — and a spine element that vanishes every morning is not spine. So it moves into the stack, hideable and movable like any card, keeping the locked row anatomy verbatim: 24×32 liquor swatch at 9px 4px 8px 5px, Shippori 16.5px name, per-origin script in Noto Serif SC, fav leaf, mono meta line. The meta line is where time and steep count sit — the locked slot, not a new column.

Empty every morning is the feature, not flicker. A diary page is blank until you write on it; the card appearing at the first cup is the page taking its first line, the same mechanism as Running low appearing when something runs low. It appears once and stays until midnight — no oscillation. The honest cost: at midnight a full tea day vanishes from Home. That is correct for a present-tense surface and it is where Sessions takes over, but it should be said rather than discovered.

No stars on it. The locked row has none either, and R2's Library consistency rule already moved ratings to detail. Time · tea · steeps is a diary line; add ★★★★½ and it is a scorecard.

9

Agreed: openSessionEdit is the wrong destination. Finding

A glance row should open session detail, which contains edit — not the edit screen, which assumes you came to change something. Tapping a diary line to look at it and landing in a form is a wrong-verb error. Note this is not scoped to the new card: recent's rows have the same wrong destination on Insights today, so fixing it there is the same fix, and R58 is where it came from.

LEFT OPEN, DELIBERATELY

Where "Sessions this week" goes. Ruling 2 removes it from the Home default set. It should default to Insights, but Insights already carries eleven cards and that is a composition question for the Insights half of R4, not this board.

Whether the greeting can be hidden. Ruling 3 makes it unhideable, which is a new constraint on shipped reorder/hide machinery. If a user has already hidden their greeting card, migration needs an answer. Flagged for Code before this ships.

Insights' Wrapped teaser, after clay. Ruling 6 takes clay off it. It is the warmest element on that surface and it should not go cold — but the replacement treatment is an Insights composition question, and settling it here would be the Home board ruling on a surface it has not drawn.

The small liquor swatch is derived, and Bundle 1 does not define it. The lock carries one geometry — the 24×32 row swatch at 9px 4px 8px 5px, used verbatim in EARLIER TODAY. Running low, Favourites and the thin shelf need a swatch that fits a text line, so they use the same 3:4 aspect and the same asymmetric radius scaled to 15×20 at 6px 3px 5px 3px. An earlier draft of this board drew them as 13×13 squares, which made one data element read two ways. A finding for the ledger: the session state records the swatch treatment as "3 sizes", but only the one size exists in Bundle 1 — so either the other two were never drawn or they live somewhere this board has not found, and the 15 rows here are waiting on that answer.

No weekly count is drawn. The export gives 42 sessions across 23 distinct days but no week boundary I can verify, so no "N this week" figure appears anywhere on this board rather than an invented one. Grams, day estimates and tea names are from the shipped library; the four square tints are placeholders, not data.
