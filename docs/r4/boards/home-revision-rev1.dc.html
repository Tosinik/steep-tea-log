Home is the present tense

Home keeps the card stack, sheds the washi band, and gets a job it can be held to. The board draws the fixed spine and four states; the stack below the spine stays the user's. Reference ids — 4a Niklas's Home · 4b the same at night · 4c day one · 4d thin.

This is a revision against a lock, not a first draft. Home was boarded in R2 WS2, deep-dived, locked at 2a, and shipped in v3.65 — DESIGN.md:6 records it. R61 protects that lock, so what follows revises where the world around Home has moved since: a fifth tab, Origins pinned to Insights, the four-lane method control, the freshness model, and a liquor swatch about to acquire a real data model. Nothing here re-opens 2a itself.

Q1 IS
ALREADY SHUT

"Is Home a card stack at all?" was answered in R2's own framing — "Home stays a user-editable card layout — so these are cards, not a fixed page" — locked with 2a and protected since by R61. Re-asking it in R4 would reverse a lock on no new evidence. The live question is the one underneath it: what does a board draw when the user composes the surface? Answer below.

A CONTRACT
BREAKS

Clay is one committing action per screen. Cards move between screens. So a card cannot carry clay: the Wrapped teaser is clay on Insights, and the moment Niklas drags it to Home it either brings a second clay onto a surface that has one, or changes appearance on arrival. Clay belongs to surfaces, not to cards. This is not a Home problem — it is a movable-card problem the contract never met, and it is load-bearing for R4's swatch work too.

The ruling

1

Home's job: what now.

Sessions is the diary, Insights is the reflection, Wrapped is the season, Teas is the shelf as objects. All of those are past tense or object tense. Home is the only present-tense surface in the app — what is ready, what is running out, what you were in the middle of. That is a job a lobby doesn't have, and it is testable.

2

The default-set rule, and its limit.

A card defaults to Home if removing it would leave you unable to answer what now without navigating. Running low passes. Favourites passes — it is what you reach for. Sessions this week fails: it is a count of what already happened, and it is the fourth default today. The rule governs the default set only; a user's move always wins and is never second-guessed by the design. Niklas has This week, mostly and Wrapped on his Home, and both are drawn here as first-class.

3

Spine and stack — what a board can draw.

The greeting is not a card. It is the masthead — fixed, unmovable, unhideable, the app's voice and the one thing every Home has in common. Everything below it is stack, and the board draws it as one configuration, labelled as one. That is the answer to "no board can draw a user-composed surface": draw the spine as specification, draw the stack as an example.

4

The washi band: dropped.

The probation contract was "if it ever fights the masthead, drop it without ceremony." Ruling 3 makes the greeting the masthead, so the band is now a second masthead gesture stacked on the first — that is the fight, arriving on schedule. Two further reasons: D's material moments survived because they carry information (liquor = identity, clay = commit) and this one was flagged from the start as carrying none; and a texture that appears on exactly one surface can never teach what it means. Home loses nothing structural. Toggle it back on in Tweaks to see what is being given up.

5

Empty is not a stack of empty cards.

Day one, every default card has nothing in it. Four empty cards would be four apologies. A card is absent until it has something to say — so day one is the greeting and one door, and cards arrive as they earn a line. R2's dashed "add a card" slots stay, but they belong to a furnished Home in edit mode, not to a bare one: an invitee should not be asked to arrange a room before they have furniture.

6

Clay lives in the spine. Cards never carry it. Revised

The earlier ruling gave Home no committing action. That was a consequence of the broken contract, not a decision, and it cost the thing the R1 mock got right — Start steeping as the screen's one commit. Corrected: each surface's spine may carry at most one clay action; a card carries none, on any surface. Home's is the greeting's suggestion, made committing. The raised Log stays jade — it is chrome, shared by every tab, and reads as a different register from content.

Why not "the surface that owns the card". That version is close and keeps Insights shipping unchanged, but it demotes a card at the moment the user promotes it: drag Wrapped onto Home — the first surface you see — and it renders quieter than it did on Insights. The user's own act of promotion makes the thing dimmer. Spine-only also removes the surprise entirely, since no card changes appearance when moved.

What it costs, and why the cost is right. Insights loses its clay: the Wrapped teaser becomes a quiet card. Clay was defined as one committing action, never selection — and a link to a reflective view commits nothing. That teaser was clay as emphasis, which is the misuse the contract existed to prevent. Insights then carries no clay at all, correctly: the reflective room has nothing to commit. Owed back: the teaser is Insights' warmest element and losing clay should not make it cold — that treatment is an Insights decision, not one to settle on the Home board.

7

Content bleeds to the edges. Every tab, not just Home. New

Porcelain paints edge to edge with no page container. The decisive reason is nesting: the stack below the greeting is already made of cards, and a card stack inside a card is a page inside a page. The masthead settles it too — a masthead is the top of a page, not an element floating on one.

Figure and ground still resolve, without a page edge: porcelain #F6F2E9 is the ground, surface #FFFEFB cards are the figure, and the masthead is ink on bare ground. That one-step token pair is what it was for. The "whiter" reading is a board artifact — the phone frames float on a dark surround so they read as objects, which the app never does.

This binds every tab. Home-only would give the app two container models, and the second one gets "fixed" later by someone who does not know it was chosen.

LEFT OPEN, DELIBERATELY

Where "Sessions this week" goes. Ruling 2 removes it from the Home default set. It should default to Insights, but Insights already carries eleven cards and that is a composition question for the Insights half of R4, not this board.

Whether the greeting can be hidden. Ruling 3 makes it unhideable, which is a new constraint on shipped reorder/hide machinery. If a user has already hidden their greeting card, migration needs an answer. Flagged for Code before this ships.

Insights' Wrapped teaser, after clay. Ruling 6 takes clay off it. It is the warmest element on that surface and it should not go cold — but the replacement treatment is an Insights composition question, and settling it here would be the Home board ruling on a surface it has not drawn.
